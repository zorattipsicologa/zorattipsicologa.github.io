Everyone has felt **anxious** at some point. It happens when we have to wake up at dawn to catch a flight, when we have to take an exam, or even when we're close to a deadline for handing in documents. The first thing we usually want to do when facing anxiety is get rid of it — remove that obstacle and calmly carry on with our commitments. At other times, we think that a certain amount of anxiety can actually energize and motivate us. {% cite wells1999trattamento %}.
So where does the truth lie?
To answer this question, we first need to understand what anxiety is. Anxiety is an emotion, and like all emotions it has a precise meaning. We feel anxious when we face situations of threat or danger over which we have little control. Anxiety is also accompanied by typical bodily sensations, such as a racing heart or excessive sweating. So what purpose does all this serve?
Mild to moderate levels of anxiety can actually help motivate us to work effectively and stay engaged. **When anxiety levels become too high, however, they negatively affect our performance and can even lead to real pathologies.** Think, for example, of episodes such as panic attacks.
The **goal** of psychological therapy is not to completely free ourselves from anxiety, but to **reduce it**, with the aim of **learning to live alongside this emotion and making use of its potential** {% cite norton2020anti %}.
